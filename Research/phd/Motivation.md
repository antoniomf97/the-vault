#phd

Artificial intelligence is undergoing a fundamental transformation in its role within human societies. AI systems are currently being developed with increasing autonomy and capabilities, leaning towards the edge of operational independence, and, further still, approaching full agency. The resulting increase in autonomy and trust leads humans to delegate increasingly open-ended tasks to AI agents, which progressively take initiative in engaging, adapting, and influencing outcomes alongside humans, rather than merely executing predefined instructions. This drastic shift in AI's role fundamentally changes human-AI relations, not just at the dyadic level, but also restructuring the social dynamics of human populations.

AI systems are becoming deeply embedded in human societies, spanning economic and institutional processes to everyday life. The recent large language model revolution marks a turning point in this trajectory, enabling the mass deployment of AI capable of complex, context-dependent interactions with humans at unprecedented scale. This progressive embedding is slowly reshaping the structure of societies, transitioning from purely human-centered systems to hybrid environments where humans and AI co-exist and collaborate within shared systems. At this scale, human-AI interactions are no longer episodic but rather systematic, with millions of simultaneous interactions shaping daily life. Alongside this, AI systems are increasingly interacting with one another, introducing a new layer of artificial-to-artificial exchanges. Together, these interactions form complex networks of humans and artificial actors from which collective patterns emerge. These patterns reshape social dynamics in ways that are difficult to predict or control.

In order to properly function cohesively, hybrid societies must rely on cooperation between humans and AI agents. This need for cooperation is not merely functional, but rather a fundamental prerequisite for the stable co-existence of agents with potentially diverging interests. Yet cooperation is far from trivial: agents compete for resources, creating an inherent tension between self-interest and collective benefit. This raises a fundamental question: how can cooperation emerge and sustain itself when agents with competing interests share the same environment? Answering this requires moving beyond population-level observations and examining how individual agents make decisions, specifically understanding their reasoning processes. While human reasoning is molded by cognitive biases and bounded rationality, AI reasoning is determined by its cognitive architecture, namely the design principles and learning processes that govern how it perceives, processes, and responds to its environment. Ultimately, it is the cognitive profile of each agent, whether human or artificial, that determines how cooperation evolves and shapes the collective dynamics of hybrid societies.

While hybrid human-AI societies are already a growing reality, their full social and philosophical implications are only beginning to unfold. As AI becomes progressively embedded across societal systems, the boundaries between human and artificial agency now begin to blur. Such a shift carries major implications, not only for how humans perceive and relate to AI at the dyadic level, but for the broader social dynamics of society as a whole. At a deeper level, this shift raises fundamental ontological questions and challenges metaphysical assumptions about the very nature of society and the mechanisms that sustain it. This underscores the urgency of fully understanding the social impact of such human-AI hybrid structures, not just to anticipate and mitigate potential conflicts, but also to guide the responsible integration of AI into human societies and ensure meaningful value alignment between human and artificial agents. This work investigates how the cognitive profiles of AI agents shape the emergence and evolution of cooperation in hybrid human-AI societies, contributing to a deeper understanding of how AI systems can be responsibly designed to promote beneficial societal outcomes.
