## Decisions taken

1. **"Unified framework" is dropped as a thesis objective.** Unification is a
   by-product of a model that happens to cover several cases, never the goal. A
   PhD that promises to unify three subfields delivers a survey.

2. **The contribution is not "cognition in EGT."** That is taken (see reading
   below). It is *cognition asymmetrically distributed across [[Species|species]]
   within one population* — humans and AI agents holding different
   [[Cognitive Profile|cognitive profiles]].

3. **The asymmetry is specified at two levels.** The AI species has neither a
   fixed strategy nor a fixed update rule. This is what stops "cognition" from
   collapsing into a relabelled conditional strategy: the update rule is not part
   of the payoff matrix, it *is* the dynamic.

4. **Selection mechanism for the AI species.** AI agents do not reproduce and do
   not imitate for fitness. Toino's position: they adopt *what is most used*, not
   what is most successful. Recorded as: **humans update on payoff, AI updates on
   frequency.** Note this is still population-level dynamics — it is conformist
   transmission, not the absence of selection. Say so explicitly or a reviewer
   will catch the slip.

## Open questions (unresolved, in priority order)

- **Does an AI agent copy what is common in the whole population, or only among
  other AI agents?** Whole-population → AI stabilises the human status quo.
  AI-only → the two dynamics decouple and can drift. This turns [[Homophily]] and
  [[Heterophily]] from definitions into the mechanism of the model.
- **Prediction, committed before simulating:** frequency-based updating amplifies
  what is already common and raises the invasion barrier for rare strategies.
  What does a growing AI share do to cooperation's *emergence* versus its
  *stability*? These may move in opposite directions. A prediction made after
  seeing the plot is worthless.
- Novelty is *not* conformity — conformist transmission is well established
  (Henrich & Boyd and successors). It is conformity in one species coupled to
  payoff updating in the other. State that precisely.
- Where does the human strategy distribution come from — assumed, calibrated, or
  empirically grounded? Still unanswered.

# Session 2 — structure of the argument

## Four gaps → one gap and two requirements

The deletion test settles it. Remove the environment and human-baseline points:
the species asymmetry still stands as a contribution. Remove the species
asymmetry: the other two are a wish-list anyone could have written.

- **The gap.** The field models AI as another human-like individual — same
  cognition, same selection mechanism. "AI cognition is abstracted away" is the
  symptom; "AI is not a distinct [[Species|species]]" is the diagnosis. One item.
- **Requirement A — environmental richness.** Not a gap. A condition for the
  effect to be *observable*: in a single well-mixed game, differing
  [[Cognitive Profile|cognitive profiles]] have almost nothing to differ about.
- **Requirement B — matched human baseline.** Not a gap. A guard against a
  **confound**: rich AI cognition vs single-rule humans conflates "AI vs human"
  with "rich vs poor model".

**Decision:** A and B move to a new **Framework** section (Background → State of
the Art → Framework). The *evidence* for them stays in the SotA — "heterophily is
unexplored in cooperation", "humans modelled with one fixed rule" are empirical
claims about the field and still need citations.

## Correction from the supervisor

## The update rule keeps drifting — watch this

Three slips in one day, each under pressure, each back toward the standard EGT
default:

1. "No population-level selection for AI" → 2. "AI follows what is most used"
(= conformist transmission, which *is* population-level) → 3. "AI copies whoever
performs best" (= payoff copying, i.e. identical to the human rule — the
asymmetry disappears).

Until the AI update rule is written down and defended, it will keep reverting.

## Species or human proxy — the open question

Toino's own [[Species]] note defines a species by shared properties. Different
memory size, reasoning cost and cognitive profile ⇒ different species, by his own
definition. The live question is therefore not *whether* but *in what respect*:

- **Strategy space:** plausibly the same. Systems trained on aggregate human
  output inherit a human-like behavioural repertoire.
- **Cognitive parameters:** different — profile, memory, cost of reasoning.
- **The loop that changes those parameters:** different, and on a different
  timescale. Humans: within-population social learning. AI (today): an outer
  design/adoption loop, selecting systems that adapt well to humans. AI
  (futuristic): possibly AI-to-AI copying.

Those last two are a natural **scenario axis** for results, not a fork to resolve
before writing.

## Also unresolved

"Helpful" ≠ "prosocial". An agent maximising its principal's payoff in a social
dilemma *defects*. If design benevolence is to be kept, it becomes a **parameter**
(prosocial bias, tunable from zero), not a background assumption.
