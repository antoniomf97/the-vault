## The question

How to organise the agent design subsection. Consensus had proposed six
dimensions: behavioural strategy, social preference, cognitive architecture,
functional role, identity/anthropomorphism, transparency/deception.

## Why the Consensus taxonomy was rejected

- **Harvested, not derived.** Labels scraped from abstracts rather than
  dimensions derived from the formal object.
- **Not orthogonal.** "Fixed vs adaptive" and "rule-based / RL / LLM" are the
  same axis named twice — a fixed strategy is the degenerate case of a learning
  rule. "Social preference" and "moral preference" are both payoff
  transformations.
- **Mixes levels.** Samaritan vs Discriminatory is filed under preference, but
  the distinction is about what the agent *conditions on*. "LLM-driven" is an
  implementation substrate, not a design dimension.
- **Mixes the agent with the human's perception of the agent.** Anthropomorphism,
  identity disclosure and interpretability enter the *human's* decision process,
  not the agent's. Nothing in an EGT model can instantiate them.

An intermediate proposal from Claude — an ⟨action space, information basis,
objective, revision rule, evolutionary coupling⟩ tuple — was **rejected by Toino
on correct grounds**: it had no citable source. Noted here because the rule
stands: a taxonomy in a proposal is either derived from the formalism in one
sentence, or cited. Never asserted.

## Why not Dafoe

Dafoe's scope is explicitly all three dyads — machine–machine, human–machine,
human–human — so it is neither machine- nor human-centric. It is
**relation-centric**: the unit of analysis is the cooperation problem *between*
parties. That is exactly why it has no reasoning dimension — a problem-oriented
decomposition enumerates what the relationship needs, not what is inside either
head.

> Our unit of analysis is the agent; Dafoe's is the relation. Different units,
> different taxonomies. Not a flaw in Dafoe, a mismatch of level.

**But cite Dafoe in [[Motivation]],** where it is strongest:
- the **horizontal vs vertical** distinction — cooperative AI is horizontal
  coordination among principals with conflicting interests; alignment is
  vertical. When alignment succeeds, the human–machine dyad reaches pure common
  interest, "an edge case in the space of cooperation problems." This is the
  canonical source for why hybrid cooperation is not an alignment problem.
- the **mixed-motive emphasis** — ML has overweighted zero-sum; the open problems
  are trust and commitment.

## The adopted frame

Adapted from the OECD Social interaction conceptual model (not the 1–5 rating
scale — the scale's unit of analysis is a deployed system, ours is a strategy in
a model).

**Dimensions:** perception · mechanism · communication · affect
**Core components:** identity · social memory
**Parked:** embodiment

Adjustments made, each to be justified in one sentence in [[Framework]]:

1. **Perception kept separate from mechanism.** Merging them collapses *what the
   agent conditions on* with *how it processes*. The thesis ladder (fixed →
   heuristic → deliberative) is a processing ladder that only means something
   with perception held constant. Jia-style opponent identification and
   Bear & Rand's intuition/deliberation are different manipulations.
2. **Embodiment parked** — principled, not convenient: identity and social memory
   have direct EGT analogues (tags/reputation; memory depth). Embodiment has
   none. Say that and the omission becomes a derivation.
3. **Consequence of (2):** affective skills split. Regulation and utilisation are
   internal and survive disembodiment. Recognition and interpretation need a
   channel — which is the communication dimension. Decide which half is kept.
4. **Adaptation is not in the frame.** In the OECD scale, learning is the
   *ordering principle of the levels*, not a dimension. Drop the levels and the
   learning axis disappears. It must be added explicitly and owned as an addition.
   In EGT the update rule *is* the reasoning process, so `mechanism` carries it —
   but name its two layers: how the agent decides *now*, and how it revises *after*.

**On citing it:** two dimensions merged, one renamed, one component dropped.
That is an adapted framework. Write "adapted from", justify each change, and
accept that the citation buys provenance, not authority.

**Watch:** in the source, `identity` means whether the agent *has* a stable
persistent self — not *which* values it holds. Loading commitment, social
preferences and moral norms into "personal values" turns identity into the
objective function and breaks the components/skills split. That would be the
third rename.

## Commitment — the answer for Han

Commitment has no single home in the frame, and that is the correct result.
Putting it under *personal values* keeps the disposition and loses the
credibility — and credibility is where the dynamics live (Schelling; Han's
arrangements with deposits and compensation are devices, not dispositions).

**It is cross-cutting, not a dimension:**
- disposition to follow through → values, under identity
- credibility → communication (a commitment is the limit case of a signal: one
  that binds)
- enforcement → institutions → handled under population/dynamics structure

Dafoe lists it at top level because a problem-oriented taxonomy enumerates
problems; ours doesn't because a mechanism-oriented one enumerates machinery.
Nothing is homeless, and the distribution is evidence for the framing.

## The scale argument

Replaces the coverage-table approach. Three moves:

1. Cognition in hybrid settings **has** been studied richly — at dyad and
   small-group scale.
2. Hybrid populations **have** been studied at scale — with cognition abstracted
   to a fixed policy.
3. The two literatures exist and do not meet.

Refinements:
- **Never** say "not studied at large scale" — we cite ten large-scale
  hybrid-population papers ourselves. The claim is the *intersection*.
- **Define what scale buys:** strategy frequencies, equilibria, tipping points,
  norm emergence, cascades through structure. None exist in a dyad. The claim is
  about what becomes invisible, not about sample size. This is also the answer to
  *why EGT rather than experiments*.
- **A team is not a small population.** Role-differentiated, goal-aligned,
  usually common-interest. Even the team literature is not studying cooperation
  under conflicting interests.
- **Do not argue transfer.** The gap claim says hybrid populations are
  structurally different (asymmetric, AI does not reproduce or imitate for
  fitness). If so, homogeneous results do not transfer. The homogeneous
  literature establishes that the dimension has **leverage**, not what will
  happen. Precisely because the sign cannot be extrapolated, it must be modelled.
  That also answers "if you already know reasoning matters, why simulate?"

## The Consensus coverage table — not usable

Rows: foundational processing, joint action mentalizing, transactive systems,
4E distributed assemblages, evolutionary dynamics. Columns: laboratory tasks,
organizational teams, large-scale simulations, field ecosystems.

- **Evolutionary dynamics is a formalism, not a cognitive mechanism.** Our row is
  miscategorised, which is why it looks sparse.
- **Columns mix method with site.** Lab tasks and simulations name a method;
  organizational teams and field ecosystems name a setting. Not mutually
  exclusive, so cell counts are meaningless.
- **Most empty cells are impossible, not unstudied.** EGT × organizational teams
  is a nonsense cell. 4E × large-scale simulation is near-empty by the theory's
  own anti-representational commitments. Labelling every blank "potential gap"
  flattens *nobody looked* and *cannot be done*.
- **The pattern is sociology of science.** Transactive memory clusters in org
  teams because that is where the construct was born.
- **14 papers total**, and our own bibliography has ~10 in the single cell it
  scores as 1.

Real names, if ever needed: behavioural/experimental economics; human-autonomy
teaming & team cognition; EGT / ABM / MARL; in-the-wild deployment studies.

**Worth keeping from it:** two rows name literatures that map onto our own core
components — *transactive memory systems* is the established literature for
`social memory`; *4E / distributed cognition* is the tradition behind the
`embodiment` we parked.

## Standing questions not yet answered

1. If the literature models AI at the equivalent of level 1 and reality is level
   2, **what actually changes in the cooperation outcome?** What is the smallest
   capability increment that flips a cooperative outcome? Without an answer this
   is a complaint, not a thesis.
2. Cognition = perception + mechanism. Which is varied in the model, and if both,
   **how are their effects told apart?**
3. "Reasoning is a choice by design" is true for AI. For humans it is an
   empirical claim. Where does the **human reasoning distribution** come from —
   assumed, calibrated, or grounded?
4. Is **affect** a dimension or an implementation? Guilt is a payoff
   transformation; anger a conditional trigger; emotion recognition is
   perception. Find one affective manipulation that changes cooperation and
   cannot be expressed as perception, communication, or objective.
