## Part 1 — Dynamics structure (28 Sep)

### Where we started

The dynamics subsection had *when* (temporal scheduling) and *how* (game protocols, horizon framing), plus a third slot, "feedback mechanisms", that stopped mid-sentence at "that is,". Question on the table: does feedback belong under *with whom*?

### Decisions

1. **"Feedback mechanisms" is dropped as a label.** It has no single meaning. What it held is re-filed:
   - institutions → their own entity (see 5)
   - norms and environmental feedback (the game changing with behaviour) → *how*
   - information structure → *how*, because a game specifies who sees what

   Information is the piece that can't be lost: [[barreda-tarrazonaExploitingMachineHuman2026|Barreda-Tarrazona2026]] (repeated pairs: humans exploit the AI) vs [[mutznerBoundedNormativeEquivalence2026|Mutzner2026]] (repeated 4-player PGG with anonymous aggregate feedback: no label effect). If Mutzner is right, partner type matters only when players can tell who did what. The two studies also differ in game and group size, so this is a hypothesis, not a finding.
2. **The Consensus sentence is rejected** ("Dynamics structure matters most because it sets when, how, and with whom agents interact"). "Matters most" is a ranking nobody has measured, and it contradicts the chapter's own opening; the "because" is circular. The when/how/with-whom triad came from that sentence, not from the model, which is why it kept colliding with population structure.
3. **CAN vs DOES (Toino's).** Population structure = who *can* interact (the network); dynamics = who *does* (the matching). Term adopted: **matching**. Random matching is the well-mixed case, assortative matching is homophily, negative assortment is heterophily (Alger & Weibull 2013). Open boundary case: rewiring ([[shiradoNetworkEngineeringUsing2020|Shirado2020]]'s bots change who *can* meet during a run). Needs a rule, e.g. "CAN is fixed for a run; anything that changes it is dynamics".
4. **Heterogeneity is composition, not matching.** It is already in population structure (intrinsic types + composition). Only homophily goes under matching.
5. **Institutions = a separate entity** that acts: rewards, punishes, matches, spends a budget. This also separates them cleanly from norms (a shared rule enforced only by the players). Where their effects land depends on what they act on: payoffs → *how*; who meets whom → matching. (Anh's institutional-incentive work frames institutions as an external decision-maker with a budget. Find the specific paper before citing.)
6. **Norms act on *how* only if shared and fixed**, and then they cannot evolve in the model. That matters if "culture" is meant to include them. Carried by individual agents and spread by copying → agent trait. What the population converges to → a result, not an input. **"Culture" is not a slot**: it is socially learned traits plus the channel they are learned through.
7. **Revision is split.** The rule (how an agent revises, and which of the available others it attends to) is agent design, in the reasoning-mechanism subsection, and stays there. Who an agent *can* learn from sits in population structure, next to who it *can* interact with. When it gets to revise goes under *when*, next to interaction timing; this is where relative update speed between species lands.
   - Criterion for every placement: Sutton & Barto's agent–environment boundary. The agent is its own machinery (policy, update rule); anything else it can't change arbitrarily is environment.
   - Consequence: some concepts split. Homophily imposed by society → dynamics; homophily as partner choice → agent design. The sections sort mechanisms, not words.
8. **No "public/private game protocol".** Use the established terms: objective vs subjective game (Kalai & Lehrer 1995). "How the individual sees the interaction" packs three things:
   - what it observes → perception
   - what it thinks the game is → its model of the game
   - what it values → preferences (the still-unnamed middle dimension of agent design: [[guoEngineeringOptimalCooperation2024|Guo2024]]'s prosocial agents)

   "Public" in the dynamics intro goes, or becomes "objective".

### What *when* should actually cite

The temporal-scheduling paragraph reports no cooperation result. The result it is reaching for is **Huberman & Glance 1993**: switching from synchronous to asynchronous updating changes spatial cooperation drastically. For a two-species population, the *when* that matters is relative update speed: **Bergstrom & Lachmann 2003** (the Red King: the slower-evolving species can come out ahead). Humans and AI were already put on different timescales on 28 Aug.

### Decisions

Each closest work lacks at least one, and the text says which. Without this paragraph, Anh reads "AI cognitive profiles" as his own Samaritan vs Discriminatory work.
2. **"Evolve" means the profile (the reasoning rule) changes**, not the strategy or learned values the rule produces. Under that definition, a Q-learner and an imitator both have fixed profiles. This distinction carries the whole gap; Quan2026 is where a reviewer will push.
3. **Evolving cognition is not new in general, only in hybrid populations.** Single-species precedents: Bear & Rand 2016 (deliberation), [[dossantosCoevolutionCooperationCognition2018|dos Santos & West 2018]] (cognition and cooperation coevolving), Kleiman-Weiner et al. 2025 (Bayesian ToM). The gap has to say why AI cognition evolves differently: the loop humans don't have (design and adoption, not imitation for payoff; 28 Aug).
4. **Scope: "both".** The gap sentence commits to AI cognitive profiles *and* their evolution. [[Abstract]] currently promises only the comparison across levels. Align before the proposal goes out.
5. **A bridge sentence can open the section** if it carries the review's real finding: the direction of the AI's effect flips with the game ([[guoFacilitatingCooperationHumanagent2023|Guo2023]]), the share of agents ([[fuOptimalIntegrationIntelligent2026|Fu2026]]) and the level of risk ([[terruchaArtCompensationHow2024|Terrucha2024]]). ### Toino's rough model idea (29 Sep)

Humans change their reasoning depending on whom they meet: meeting a higher reasoner → a chance to imitate that profile; meeting AI → a chance of more conformism.

- **It evolves human profiles, not the AI's**, while the gap sentence says the opposite. It may be the better thesis (AI changing how humans reason, at population scale; [[riedlCognitiveSpilloverHuman2026|Riedl2026]] shows exposure effects in teams). Check it the same way (who tried it, where they stopped) and decide before polishing either.
- **Observability.** Actions are visible, reasoning is not. To imitate a profile, humans must perceive it. Say how.
- **"AI → conformism" is assumed.** Riedl2026 shows that exposure changes shared language and mental models in teams, not a switch to conformist learning. If it stays assumed, it is a scenario you vary, not a mechanism you claim.
- **Neither flow depends on payoff.** That is contagion, not selection: the reasoning mix is set by who meets whom, and cooperation just reads it out. Defensible as Riedl's exposure effect, but say so. Bear & Rand's result depends on deliberation spreading only when it pays more than it costs.

This belongs in [[Framework]], written down as a rule.
