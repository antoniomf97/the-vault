## Slide 2: motivation (12:06)

**Toino's draft:** (1) AI more embedded in society → (2) society's good function requires human–AI cooperation → (3) natural cooperation depends on how AI reasons → (4) we have to investigate how reasoning and its evolution affect future societies.

**Diagnosis.** The shape is right; two boxes break it.
- Box 3 states the thesis's answer as a premise. Whether AI reasoning matters for cooperation *at population scale* is the gap, so it can't be assumed on slide 2.
- Box 4 is the aim, not a motivation, and "evolution" arrives with no premise behind it.
- Boxes 1 and 2 are too soft: "embedded" doesn't need cooperation (a spreadsheet is embedded), and "good function" names no tension.

**What each box has to establish** (the wording is yours):

| Box | Must establish | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Participant | AI acts, not just executes, and at scale | Rahwan2019, Han2026; one number: Chatterji2025 (~10% of adults by July 2025) |
| 2. Dilemma | Participants with diverging interests share resources, so cooperation is not guaranteed. Not the same problem as alignment | Dafoe2020, Dafoe2021 |
| 3. Reasoning matters, in dyads | How an LLM agent reasons changes how well it coordinates with human players | Akata2025 (social chain-of-thought; repeated 2×2 games) |
| 4. Reasoning changes, on both sides | Exposure to AI changes how people think; AI is retrained on its users' data | Riedl2026; Pedreschi2025 |
| → bottom line | One question, not "we have to investigate" | — |

**Why this order.** Box 3 is the dyad side, and slide 3 (the puzzle: fixed agents shift cooperation, direction flips) is the population side. Together they foreshadow the gap ("the two bodies of work stop on opposite sides"). Boxes 3 and 4 map onto the gap's two halves and onto RQ1 vs RQ2–3.

**Trap.** Riedl2026 is teams, and its effects are on language, attention and cohesion. It supports "exposure changes how people think", not "humans change update rule". Same warning as Shin2023 on 9 Oct.

**Hard question.** Box 2: why cooperation, and not alignment or welfare, as the outcome? Someone on the committee will ask.
